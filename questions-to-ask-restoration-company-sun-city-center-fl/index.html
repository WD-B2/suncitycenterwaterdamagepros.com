Questions to Ask a Restoration Company, Sun City Center, FL.

A few direct questions separate a clear plan from a vague promise. Ask them on the first call.

By the Sun City Center Water Damage Pros team

Call (813) 738-0233

When water is on the floor, you call whoever answers. That is fine for the first call, but it helps to know what to ask. If water is still coming in, call our 24/7 line at (813) 738-0233 and a same-day response is available when a crew is free.

These questions work for any company, including ours. They focus on scope, price, equipment and records, because those are the things that go wrong.

The First Five Questions.

  1. What will you do on day one, and what will it cost? Ask for scope and price before work starts.
  2. What equipment will you place, and for how many days?
  3. Will you take moisture readings and share them every day?
  4. Do your crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard?
  5. What do you do if the readings do not fall as planned?

A clear company answers these in plain words. If the answers sound like sales talk, ask again. The point is to hear a plan.

Write down the answers. They are easy to compare when two companies call you back.

Red Flags and Better Signs.

Red flag.Better sign.
No scope or price before work.Written scope and price explained first.
Pressure to sign right now.Time to read what you sign.
Promises about what the insurer will pay.Clear statement that the insurer decides coverage.
No readings, only "we will dry it".Daily readings you can see.
Cannot say what gets removed.List of materials to dry and to remove.
Vague about licensing for mold work.Clear roles for licensed assessors and remediators.

Be careful with any company that promises an insurance outcome. Coverage depends on the policy and the insurer. A company can document the loss, but it cannot decide the claim.

Also be careful with anything that asks you to sign over insurance rights before you understand it. Read it, and ask your insurer what it means.

Questions About Mold.

If growth is possible, ask who will assess it and who will remove it. Florida licenses mold assessors and remediators through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation under Chapter 468, Part XVI, and separates the roles on one project.

A company that tests and removes on the same job is a flag in Florida. See mold remediation for how it should work.

Local Details Worth Asking About.

Ask whether the crew knows how storms affect this area. The Little Manatee River near Wimauma at US 301 has a flood stage of 11 feet, and Hillsborough County issued flood warnings there during Debby in August 2024 and Milton in October 2024. After Helene in September 2024, the county reported damage to wastewater pumping systems in Apollo Beach and Ruskin.

Ask how fast a crew can reach you in a busy week. A company that promises an exact arrival time is promising something no one can control. A same-day response, when a crew has an opening, is an honest statement.

Questions About Contents and Rebuild.

  • Do you handle contents, such as cleaning or moving furniture?
  • Will you remove drywall and carpet, and who disposes of it?
  • Do you rebuild, or do I hire a contractor?
  • How will you document the loss for my insurer?
  • What happens if I find more damage later?

Rebuild is its own phase. Some companies do it and some hand it off. Ask which, and get the price before work starts.

We dry and restore. For rebuild we explain what is needed and let you pick the contractor, unless we agree on a scope together.

What a Good Call Sounds Like.

A good first call asks about the source, the time, the rooms and the water type. It tells you what to do while you wait, such as shutting off the main and staying out of wet rooms near power.

Crews serving Wimauma and Apollo Beach start the same way. Read structural drying to see what readings and logs look like before you compare quotes.

Fill in the table for each quote. A lower price with fewer days of equipment may mean the job is not finished when the equipment leaves. A higher price with more detail may be the better value.

Ask each company why its number is what it is. Honest answers come with reasons, not pressure.
