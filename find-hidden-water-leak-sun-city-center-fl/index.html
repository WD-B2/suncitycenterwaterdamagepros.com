Find a Hidden Water Leak in Sun City Center, FL Homes.

Hidden leaks reveal themselves in the bill, the meter and the walls. These checks help you find them before damage spreads.

By the Sun City Center Water Damage Pros team

Call (813) 738-0233

A hidden leak does not announce itself. It shows up as a bill that climbs, a floor that feels warm or a smell that will not go away. If water is still coming in, call our 24/7 line at (813) 738-0233 and a same-day response is available when a crew is free.

These simple checks cost nothing and help you decide whether to call a plumber, a restoration crew or both.

The Meter Test.

  1. Turn everything off.

    Shut off faucets, washers, dishwashers and ice makers. Make sure no one flushes.

    .

  2. Read the meter.

    Look at the leak indicator, a small triangle or dial, or note the digits.

    .

  3. Wait 30 minutes.

    Do not use any water.

    .

  4. Read again.

    If the indicator moves or digits change, water is leaking somewhere after the meter.

    .

If the meter moves, close the main valve at the house and check again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.

Hillsborough County Water Resources can help with questions about the meter and the public side of the line.

Toilet and Appliance Checks.

Drop a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a flapper leak. Pull out the washing machine and check hoses and the drain box. Look under sinks and behind the refrigerator.

Small drips cost money and also feed hidden damage. See appliance leak water damage and water heater leak cleanup for common sources.

Why Slab Homes Hide Leaks.

Many homes in Sun City Center sit on concrete slabs, so supply lines can run in or under the concrete. A leak there has no place to drip, so it moves sideways into carpet and wall bases. The community was founded in 1961 and has about 18,000 housing units, and pipes in older ones have years of service.

We cannot cite a local count of slab leaks. What we can describe is the mechanism, which is why warm spots, a spinning meter and damp carpet are the signs to watch.

Who to Call.

  • Plumber: locates and repairs the leaking line or fixture.
  • Restoration crew: extracts water and dries the structure.
  • Roofer: closes roof openings.
  • HVAC technician: fixes condensate drains and pans.
  • Licensed assessor: handles suspected mold.

The plumber and the drying crew do different jobs. Call both when water has reached the floor or walls. Our crew works with your plumber, not instead of one.

When Water Has Already Spread.

If you find damp carpet, soft baseboards or a stain, call for readings. A meter and a thermal camera can map the wet area without tearing out. Drying early is far cheaper than repairing later.

See structural drying for how readings guide the work.

A Seven-Point Walkthrough.

  1. Read the meter with everything off and watch for movement.
  2. Check toilet tanks with a few drops of food coloring.
  3. Look under every sink for drips, stains and swollen cabinet floors.
  4. Inspect the water heater base and the pan under it.
  5. Pull the washer out and check hoses and the drain box.
  6. Walk the outside perimeter for soggy ground or a hissing sound.
  7. Press on baseboards and look at ceilings for new stains.

Write down what you find and the time. If the meter moves and you cannot find a source, a plumber can use listening equipment, pressure tests or a camera. Those tools find the pipe. They do not dry the damage.

When water has reached walls or floors, readings show how far it went. Our 24/7 line takes those calls, and a same-day response is available when a crew is free.

Outdoor Leaks That Feed Indoor Damage.

A broken irrigation line, a cracked supply pipe and a clogged gutter can all push water toward the foundation. Soggy soil beside the house, a patch of green grass in dry weather or a constant hiss can point to a line.

Water against a slab edge can wick into wall bases. If you see damp baseboards on an exterior wall after rain or watering, read storm water damage cleanup for how outside water is handled.
