Prevent Water Damage While Away in Sun City Center, FL.

A leak that runs for a week in an empty house does far more harm than one caught in an hour. A short checklist cuts the risk.

By the Sun City Center Water Damage Pros team

Call (813) 738-0233

Many people in this area leave their homes for weeks or months in the summer. A leak while you are away can run unnoticed until the damage is serious. If water is still coming in, call our 24/7 line at (813) 738-0233 and a same-day response is available when a crew is free.

These steps do not cost much and they reduce the odds of a big loss. Check your insurance policy too, since some policies set rules for homes left vacant.

Before You Leave: The Core Checklist.

  1. Shut off the main water valve at the house, or at least at the meter box.
  2. Turn off the water heater or set it to vacation mode.
  3. Close supply valves to washers and dishwashers.
  4. Clean air conditioner drain lines and confirm the pan is clear.
  5. Set the air conditioner to a steady temperature and humidity setting.
  6. Clear gutters and downspouts and check the roof edge.
  7. Give a trusted person a key and the plumber's number.

Walk through the house once more and look under sinks and around the water heater. A small drip you spot today will not be a flood next month.

See water heater leak cleanup and AC leak water damage for the two sources that fail most often in empty homes.

What to Shut Off and What to Leave On.

System.Away setting.Why.
Main water valve.Off.Stops supply to every pipe.
Water heater.Off or vacation mode.Prevents heating a tank with no use.
Air conditioner.On, steady setting.Controls humidity and prevents damp growth.
Irrigation timer.Check or pause.A broken head can run all week.
Washer and dishwasher valves.Closed.Hoses are a common failure point.

Leave the air conditioner running even when you are away. Turning it off lets humidity climb, and closed rooms can develop musty air within weeks in Florida summers.

If you shut the main valve, remember that fire sprinklers, if any, may be on a separate line. Ask your installer.

Summer Storms While You Are Gone.

The National Weather Service in Tampa Bay reports that 55 to 70 percent of the area's rain falls from June through September. Those are the months many residents are away. A roof leak or a blocked gutter during a storm has no one to catch it.

Arrange for a neighbor or a property check service to walk through after storms. Hillsborough County storm information and flood zone tools are on the county website, and Hillsborough County Water Resources can help with water service questions.

Tools That Help.

  • Leak sensors near the water heater, sinks, washer and air handler.
  • A smart shutoff valve that closes when it senses a leak.
  • Phone alerts from the sensors.
  • A plumber on call who has your key information.
  • A neighbor or service who can enter within a day.

Sensors are inexpensive compared with a flooded house. Place them where water collects first. Test them before you leave.

Share the sensor alert with someone local. An alert on your phone does not help if you cannot reach the house.

If You Come Home to Water.

Shut off the main if it is still on. Stay out of rooms with water near outlets. Take photos, then call. We take calls at any hour, and a same-day response is available when a crew is free.

Read water extraction for what happens first. Crews in Balm and Apollo Beach see this after summer trips more than any other time.

Insurance and Vacancy.

Some policies limit coverage for water damage if a home is vacant for a stated period, or require the water to be shut off. Read the vacancy and unoccupancy language, or ask your agent before you go.

Keep a list of your steps with the date. It shows you took reasonable care if a loss happens.

A Month-by-Month Away Routine.

When.What to check.Who.
Before you leave.Shut-offs, drain lines, gutters, sensors.You.
After the first week.Walk-through for drips, odor, alerts.Neighbor or service.
After each major storm.Roof edge, windows, floors, air handler pan.Neighbor or service.
Before you return.Run water slowly, check under sinks, look at ceilings.You.

Open the main valve slowly when you return and check for hissing or drips. Run each faucet and flush each toilet, then look under sinks. A small leak caught on day one is easy to fix.

If the house smells musty, ceilings look stained or carpet feels damp, call for readings. Our 24/7 line takes calls at any hour, and a same-day response is available when a crew has an opening.

Garages, Lanais and Outbuildings.

Water heaters in garages, washers in utility rooms and air handlers in closets are all places a leak can hide. Check each one. Look at the floor and at the walls behind them.

Lanais and screened porches take blowing rain. Move rugs and cushions up and out. A wet pad on a porch floor can grow growth in weeks. For storm damage, read storm water damage cleanup.
