Warm Floor, Wet Carpet or a Water Bill Spike? That May Be a Slab Leak.

If part of the floor feels warm, the carpet is damp with no source, or you hear water running when everything is off, a pipe under the concrete may be leaking. Plumbers call that a slab leak, and the drying work that follows is what we do.

A slab leak becomes an emergency when water reaches the floor surface or soaks the carpet and walls. Shut off the main valve, call a plumber to locate the line and call our 24/7 line for extraction and drying. A same-day response is available when a crew is free.

Slab leak water damage is the wetting of flooring, wall bases and the slab edge after a pressurized water line leaks in or beneath a concrete foundation. It matters because the water has nowhere to drain, so it moves sideways into every room.

What We Do Versus What the Plumber Does.

Finding a leak under concrete takes acoustic listening gear, line pressure tests or a camera, and that is plumbing work. Repair options include spot repairs through the slab or rerouting the line overhead. Ask your plumber to explain both.

Our part is the water. The crew extracts what is on the floor, lifts wet carpet and pad, checks the slab edge and wall bases with moisture meters, and dries the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers. Read structural drying for how drying is confirmed.

What to Do While You Wait on a Slab Leak.

  • Close the main water valve.
  • Turn off the water heater if the leak may be on the hot line.
  • Lift rugs and furniture off damp floors.
  • Do not cut into the slab yourself.
  • Photograph wet carpet, warm spots and the meter reading.

What affects the cost of slab leak cleanup.

The cleanup cost depends on how long the leak ran, how many rooms are wet and what flooring was laid over the slab. Tile can often be saved, while carpet and pad usually need to be replaced.
