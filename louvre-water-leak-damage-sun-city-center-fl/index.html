Louvre Window Water Leak Damage in Sun City Center, FL.

Louvre windows and doors let air through by design. In wind-driven rain, that design can let water in too.

By the Sun City Center Water Damage Pros team

Call (813) 738-0233

Louvre windows, also called jalousie windows, use angled glass slats that open and close. They give good airflow, and they are common on porches, enclosed lanais and older homes. They also leak more easily than a sealed window when rain is driven sideways. If water is still coming in, call our 24/7 line at (813) 738-0233 and a same-day response is available when a crew is free.

This post explains how that water moves, what it damages and how to stop it from becoming a bigger problem.

Why Louvre Windows Leak.

Each slat overlaps the next, but the overlap is not a seal. Wind pushes rain through the gaps. Worn gaskets, bent frames and slats that no longer close tightly make it worse.

Louvre doors and vents work the same way. Water that comes in at the sill can run along the floor or into the wall base.

Where the Water Goes.

Water that enters a louvre window can run down the wall cavity, soak the baseboard and wick into the floor. Because the first drip is small, people often wipe it and move on.

Repeated wetting is what causes damage. Wood trim swells, paint bubbles and drywall softens from the bottom. Read wall and drywall water damage repair to see how a wall is dried or cut.

Louvre Windows in a Retirement Community.

Sun City Center was founded in 1961, and many homes in the community date from the decades after. Older Florida rooms and lanais often include louvre or jalousie glass.

We cannot cite a count of how many homes here have them, so we stick to the mechanism. Where they exist, they deserve a check before the summer rain season, which the National Weather Service in Tampa Bay places from June through September.

Prevention Before the Next Storm.

  • Close and lock louvre windows before a forecast storm.
  • Check slats for chips and gaskets for cracks.
  • Clear tracks and weep holes of dirt and leaves.
  • Move furniture and rugs away from windows in heavy rain.
  • Ask a contractor about replacing worn units.

If water has already reached the floor, call. Crews in Wimauma and Balm handle these calls with meters and air movers.

Do not caulk over a wet frame. Sealing in moisture can lead to hidden rot and mold. Dry first, then repair.

Louvre Doors, Vents and Porch Walls.

Louvre doors on closets, utility rooms and porches can leak the same way windows do. Louvre vents on gable ends and soffits let air in, and in a strong storm they may let rain in too.

Water that enters an attic through a vent can soak insulation and drip through the ceiling. If you see a ceiling stain after wind-driven rain, read ceiling water damage repair.

Louvre Windows Versus Sealed Windows.

Feature.Louvre or jalousie.Sealed or impact window.
Airflow.Very high when open.Low or none when fixed.
Rain resistance.Lower in wind-driven rain.Higher when installed well.
Maintenance.Gaskets and hardware wear.Seals and caulk need checks.
Typical risk.Water at the sill and wall base.Failed seals or flashing.

Neither type is perfect. A sealed window with failed flashing can leak into the wall for years without a visible drip. A moisture reading is the best check for either.

If you plan to replace windows, ask the installer how the flashing and wall opening will be sealed. The water damage we see around windows often traces to the opening, not the glass.

Drying a Wall Below a Leaking Window.

If the wall below a louvre window is wet, the crew removes the baseboard, reads the drywall and decides whether to dry in place or cut. Small holes at the base let dry air in. Air movers and a dehumidifier do the rest.

Dry first and repair second. New caulk over wet drywall only locks moisture in. Our line is open 24/7 if the leak happened during a storm and the floor is still wet.
